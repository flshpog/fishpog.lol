Correct entry:

Wrong entry (what was originally written):

Why it's wrong: crediting Amy's A/R for $3,100 when she only owes $2,600 sends her subledger balance to −$500. A customer can't owe negative money - that would mean you owe her, which isn't true.

3. Daily vs Monthly posting - the rule

Does the GL account have its own dedicated column in a special journal?
  • YES → posted monthly (column total only, at month-end)
  • NO - it's in "Other Accounts" → posted daily (each line individually)
  • In the General Journal → posted daily (both Dr and Cr legs)

Why it works this way:

  • High-volume accounts (Cash, A/R, A/P, Sales, MI) get dedicated columns so you batch them up. One posting per month instead of 50.
  • Occasional accounts (Rent, Utilities, Equipment) sit in "Other Accounts." The column total there is meaningless (mixed accounts) - that's why it gets (X) and lines get posted individually.
  • GJ entries are always unusual (returns, adjustments) and have no column system, so they post immediately.

4. What goes in the General Journal

Anything that doesn't fit in one of the four special journals.

The 4 special journals only handle:

  • Sales Journal → credit sales only
  • Cash Receipts → anything where cash comes in
  • Purchases Journal → credit purchases only
  • Cash Disbursements → anything where cash goes out

Common GJ entries:

  1. Sales returns from customers
  2. Purchase returns to suppliers
  3. Adjusting entries (depreciation, accruals, supplies used)
  4. Closing entries (year-end)
  5. Correcting entries
  6. Owner contributions/withdrawals of non-cash assets
  7. Any transaction with no cash AND no credit sale/purchase

5. "Which journal?" decision test

Run through these in order - first match wins:
  1. Does it involve cash coming in? → Cash Receipts Journal
  2. Does it involve cash going out? → Cash Disbursements Journal
  3. Is it a credit sale of merchandise? → Sales Journal
  4. Is it a credit purchase? → Purchases Journal
  5. Otherwise → General Journal
